Pilgrimage to Washtucna

Washtucna, May 4–6, 2026

Where is this place?

Where you can see the stars
Almost too many.

Passing
Miles of yellow canola
And green lentil sprouts
Color drifting
Into brown sagebrush
And dusty gravel side roads

A lone modest house
Perched in the crux
Of rolling hills

“This is the place.”
“This is the place?”

Cell phones don’t work
Just a group of cattle
Gathered near the top of the ridge
Discussing something important
Tumbleweeds and basalt
Become twilight. And disappear.

A rattlesnake on a warm stone
Flicks his tongue
Tasting cool night

Where you can see the stars.
God, it’s too many.
Mouth hanging open, staring upward.

Satellites tick by overhead
On course.
Our necks hurt.
Still we gaze.

“What trillionaire owns those satellites?”
We laugh.
Then sigh.
I contemplate
“Who owns this dust?”

The only light
Not radiating
Trillionaire heavens
Is the yellow glow
From a small nightlight
In the upstairs bedroom
Of the little house.

In the middle of nowhere
Washtucna.

It’s way too late
And there we are
Still standing out
On the gravel road

Rapt.

“Who owns the dust?”
Everyone has a different story.

And we finally begin to shuffle
Toward the yellow light.
Tired eyes and chilled fingers.
The rattlesnake uncurls
And slides away into the shadows.

Into Washtucna.
